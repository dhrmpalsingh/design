# FLUTTER — The Creature

**Status:** Proposed lock v0.1. This is the most important visual-development document in the project. Lock it before any design generation.

> Not a normal butterfly. Not a robot shaped like a butterfly. **Something genuinely new: nature and machinery figuring out how to coexist.**

**Design north star:** It should look **fragile, almost accidental.** It must NOT look like sophisticated technology. If a frame of it could be used in an advert for a gadget, the design has failed.

**Names:**
- Sameer never names it on screen.
- The Authority's designation, seen on the ops screen and the evidence vial: **ORGANISM 41-786-A**.
- Production nickname: open (see Decisions).

---

## 1. Origin: how it came to exist

Sameer **is not trying to create a butterfly.** It happens by accident. There are three ingredients.

| Ingredient | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **The pollination unit** | A **PU-7 micro-pollinator**: insect-scale, with carbon-fibre wing spars, micro-segmented graphite spine, sterile engineered tissue, and a **self-repair gel** that regrows damaged tissue from a genetic template broadcast by the facility network | The disposal tray on a Contained agricultural floor. It's damaged, with **one bent right forewing spar.** |
| **The preserved biology** | Grandmother's vial: **lepidopteran germ cells / eggs in cryoprotectant**, pre-Collapse, still viable | The inherited tin. Sameer thinks it's spore or seed stock. |
| **The habitat** | Soil, condensation water, lamp warmth, and **CO₂ and moisture from his breath** | The hidden bay |

### What happened (in-world logic, never explained on screen)

On Day 0, desperate to revive the dying soil, Sameer cracks the pollination unit open, squeezes its repair gel into the soil as a nutrient, empties the vial in alongside it, and buries the lot.

The repair gel is designed to **rebuild the unit from a template.** Cut off from the facility network, it gets no template signal, so it takes one from the nearest viable genetic material: **the butterfly germ cells.** It doesn't "reproduce." It **repairs itself into something else**, absorbing the unit's mechanical parts as scaffolding and growing new biology around them.

> **The difference between healing and reproducing turned out to be smaller than anyone had planned for.**

That one line is the creature's whole scientific premise. The design team, writers and anyone generating images should understand it. The audience never needs to hear it.

## 3. The chrysalis

- **Size:** About 3.5 cm long, hanging under the greying strawberry leaf.
- **Surface:** **Translucent organic tissue**, slightly wet, like the skin of a grape held up to light.
- **Underneath:** **Fine metallic threads.** The unit's carbon fibre and filament have been repurposed as silk, running in uneven lines beneath the tissue like veins, or wiring.
- **Attachment:** The silk pad that anchors it to the leaf is part organic thread, part frayed filament.
- **Life signs:** Condensation beads on it. In one shot only, a **barely perceptible pulse.**
- **What Sameer thinks it is:** Mould, a fungal growth fused with the dead unit's parts. A failure. He has seen a hundred failures.
- **Do not:** No glow, no LEDs, no clean seams. It should look like something that might be rotting.

---

## 4. Anatomy of the adult

Overall size: **a large butterfly, 10–11 cm wingspan.**

| Part | Organic or mechanical | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Head** | Mostly organic | Small. Compound eyes, **dark and organic, not camera lenses.** Delicate antennae, one slightly shorter than the other. |
| **Proboscis** | Organic | A coiled feeding tube. It uncoils to drink condensation. |
| **Thorax** | **Hybrid** | Organic flight muscle wrapped around **microscopic mechanical vertebrae**: tiny graphite segments from the unit's spine, visible through thin tissue like a fish skeleton seen through skin. |
| **Abdomen** | **Fully organic** | Soft, pale, segmented and slightly translucent. It visibly expands and contracts when it breathes. It's the most *animal* part of it. |
| **Legs** | Organic | Six, thin and jointed, with tiny hooked feet for gripping leaves and skin |
| **Wings** | **Hybrid** | A **translucent membrane** with **visible vascular structures**: branching veins carrying faint smoky-amber fluid. They're supported by **tiny carbon-fibre spars** that run alongside the veins and in places replace them. |
| **Surface** | Organic | Fine scales that **shed**. Moisture collects on the body and wings. |

### Asymmetry (lock this; it's the creature's signature)

- **The right forewing has a kinked spar** that it inherited from the damaged unit. The right wing is slightly shorter and a little creased.
- As a result, **its flight dips to the right**, a small, consistent list.
- The antennae are uneven. The vein pattern differs left to right.
- **This asymmetry is how the audience recognises its descendant in the coda** (see §8).

### Colour and light

- **No glow. No LEDs. No electric blue.** No light comes from the creature at all.
- The wings are clear to smoky, like wet onion skin, with **faint iridescence only at certain angles** (a hint of rose-gold that picks up the amber sky).
- The veins are pale amber. The vertebrae are matte graphite. The abdomen is pale cream.
- It **takes its colour from the light around it**, so it looks different in lamp light, window light and scan light.

## 5. The emergence

1. The chrysalis **splits** along an uneven seam, tearing organic tissue and snapping filament.
2. The creature **pulls itself out**: wet, crumpled, wings folded like a closed umbrella.
3. It **hangs and pumps fluid into its wings.** The veins visibly fill. The wings slowly expand and flatten. *(This is real butterfly behaviour. Keep it.)*
4. Moisture beads along its body. It **rests.** The abdomen breathes.
5. **The right wing doesn't fully flatten.** The kink stays.

---

## 6. Flight & behaviour

### First flight (imperfect by design)

1. It tries to take off and **crashes** into the bay wall.
2. **A wing sticks** to the wet condensation jar. It pulls free.
3. It **rests** on Sameer's sleeve, wings trembling.
4. Then it **flutters.** It's uneven, dipping right and erratic. It's the first un-designed movement in the film.

As it passes through the lamp, **shed scales drift in the light beam**, making the beam visible for the first time in the film (see the light rule in World Rules §11).

### Ongoing behaviour

| Behaviour | Why | Story use |
|---|---|---|
| **Attracted to breath** (CO₂ and moisture) | Flowers emit CO₂; it reads breath as "flower" | It lands near Sameer's mouth. Intimacy. |
| **Drinks condensation** | It needs water constantly; the filtered 45% humidity air dries it out | It drinks from his respirator reservoir: **it lives on his breath** |
| **Drawn to light and movement** | Phototaxis | It goes to the window, then to the drone scan lights. **It becomes hard to hide.** |
| **Seeks warm, humid enclosed spaces** | Egg-laying instinct | It disappears into the exhaust duct (Day 15) |
| **Rests often** | Fragile, with limited energy | Pacing: quiet beats |
| **Does not respond to signals, commands or patterns** | No network template. It's free. | **The thematic core:** the first thing in the film that isn't following a design |

### What it cannot survive

- **Perfect city air**: too dry and too clean, with no sugars. Left in a normal apartment, it would die in about a day.
- **It needs imperfection to live:** humidity, CO₂, condensation, the lamp, Sameer's breath. *The habitat is fragile, and so is the creature.*

---

## 7. Reproduction

- **Parthenogenetic.** It reproduces **without a mate.** That's how one creature can be the start of everything, and it's the Authority's worst fear: **one is enough.**
- **Eggs:** Tiny, pearl-like, about 1 mm, each with a **single metallic fleck.** They're laid loosely in the condensation film of warm, humid enclosed spaces.
- **Where:** On Day 15, inside the **exhaust duct** Sameer routed the habitat's humidity into. From there, airflow carries some of them up the shared riser into the inter-tower air exchange.
- **The Authority never finds them.** The drone sweep (Beat 31) passes the duct junction and moves on. The film leaves that ambiguous.

### Generational drift: nature taking over

The mechanical parts **can't be fully inherited**: there are no more pollination units to scavenge. Biology can be inherited. So **each generation is more organic than the last.**

| | Generation 1 (Sameer's) | Generation 2 (coda) |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 10–11 cm | 7–8 cm |
| Mechanical vertebrae | Visible along the thorax | **Vestigial**: two or three graphite flecks |
| Carbon-fibre spars | Several | **None**: organic veins only |
| Wing tone | Clear and smoky | **First faint natural pigment**, a soft dusk-blue shadow at the wing edges |
| Asymmetry | Right-wing kink, dips right | **Still dips slightly right**, an inherited trait from a broken machine |

The audience recognises the coda creature by **the dip to the right.** It doesn't need dialogue to say "it's hers."

## 8. Detection signatures

What the city's sensors actually see. Everything here has to stay consistent with the panel readings in the beat sheet.

| Signature | Sensor | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Humidity bloom | Humidity | The habitat needs more moisture once the creature exists, and it leaks from the bay |
| CO₂ drawdown | O₂/CO₂ | The plant consumes CO₂. Sameer masks this by feeding it from his breath line (Beat 21). |
| Micro-motion at **8–14 Hz** | Acoustic / micro-motion | Real butterflies beat at 5–12 Hz and the PU-7 at about 200 Hz. **It falls between, so it can't be classified as a licensed device or a known organism.** Result: **UNIDENTIFIED ORGANIC MOTION.** |
| Pollen | Particulate | **The fatal signature.** Reproduction bypasses every threshold. |
| Missing unit | *Not a sensor; bureaucracy* | Preservation's disposal reconciliation is **one PU-7 short.** The last handler on the service log is Sameer. |

## 9. The Authority's threat assessment (why their fear is rational)

What the Preservation ops screen shows in Beat 23. It's text only and never read aloud.

> **ORGANISM 41-786-A**
> Classification: **AUTONOMOUS**
> Reproductive mode: **PARTHENOGENETIC (probable)**
> Dispersal: **AIRBORNE**
> Ecological function: **POLLINATOR**: enables reproduction in other organisms
> Origin: **HYBRID**: licensed PU-7 infrastructure + unregistered germline
> Containment risk: **CROSS-CONTAMINATION OF CONTAINED AGRICULTURE**
> Historical profile match: **REMEDY-CLASS ORGANISM — 87%**

In plain terms, from their side: *it's airborne, it needs no mate, it helps other things reproduce, and it's built from our own pollination tech, so it could get into the food facilities.* **It's the thing that ended the world, again.** The audience should feel that they might be right.

## 10. Design do's & don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Fragile, translucent, wet, a little wrong | Sleek, glossy, symmetrical, "premium" |
| Mechanical parts **under** tissue, glimpsed | Exposed circuitry, visible chips, screws on the surface |
| Organic dominates; machine is the skeleton | Machine dominates; organics are decoration |
| Irregular asymmetry with a consistent right-wing kink | Perfect mirror symmetry |
| Natural butterfly movement: rests, crashes, flutters | Drone movement: hovering, precise turns, smooth paths |
| Takes colour from its surroundings | Emits light, glows, has an energy colour |
| Moisture, shed scales, a breathing abdomen | Dry, clean, sealed |
| Looks like it might die at any moment | Looks durable or heroic |

## 11. Canonical description *(for generation consistency)*

Use this as the base text for every design and production generation of the creature, then add shot-specific detail after it.

> *A fragile hybrid butterfly, 10 cm wingspan. Translucent, wet, smoky wing membranes with visible branching pale-amber veins, supported by a few hair-thin matte carbon-fibre spars running alongside the veins. The right forewing is slightly shorter with a kinked spar and a crease. Soft, pale, cream-coloured segmented organic abdomen, slightly translucent. A thin thorax where tiny graphite vertebrae are visible beneath thin organic tissue, like a fish skeleton through skin. Dark organic compound eyes, uneven antennae. Moisture beads on the body. No glow, no lights, no visible electronics. Faint rose-gold iridescence only at angles. Delicate, imperfect, almost accidental — nature and machinery coexisting.*

**Generation 2 variant:** *Same lineage, smaller (7 cm), fully organic veins with no carbon spars, only two or three tiny graphite flecks along the thorax, a faint dusk-blue pigment at the wing edges, and still a slight right-wing asymmetry.*

**Chrysalis:** *A 3.5 cm chrysalis hanging under a greying strawberry leaf. Translucent wet organic tissue like grape skin held to light, with fine uneven metallic threads running beneath it like veins or wiring. The anchoring silk pad is part organic thread, part frayed filament. Condensation beads. No glow. It looks like it might be rotting.*
