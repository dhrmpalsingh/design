# FLUTTER — Decisions to Lock

These are the open choices that need sign-off before dialogue (if any) or any production generation. Each one has a recommendation. Where an option is marked **(rec.)**, the other four documents already assume it.

---

## A. Story & structure

### 1. Dialogue: none?
- **(rec.) No spoken dialogue.** All words are interface text.
- Alternative: minimal dialogue (a work call, a neighbour).

**Why I recommend none:** Ila lives alone and the antagonist is a building, so there's nobody natural to talk to. A wordless film travels across festivals without subtitles. And for AI-assisted production it **sidesteps lip-sync entirely**, which is still the weakest part of generated video. The "dialogue" pass becomes an **interface-text pass**: every panel line, notice and screen readout written and locked. That's a much smaller, more controllable job.

### 2. Is the Authority right?
- **(rec.) Keep it ambiguous.** The ending can be read as hope *or* outbreak. The threat assessment (Creature §9) is factually correct within the world.
- Alternative: tilt clearly toward hope.

**Why:** The rational fear is what makes the creature *mean* something. If the Authority is just wrong, the creature is just cute.

### 3. Ila's one active choice
- **(rec.) The sterilisation-port refusal (Beat 32).** It gives her agency without heroics: the most radical act available to her is to *not kill something*.
- Alternatives: she releases it into the duct herself (more active, but it contradicts "we think it's captured" and makes her a rebel), or she has no final choice (more passive, with a weaker climax).

### 4. How the next generation reaches another tower
- **(rec.) Eggs laid in the exhaust duct she routed humidity into (B21), carried through the shared riser and the inter-tower air exchange that runs through the bridges.** Her attempt to hide the habitat is exactly what spreads it.
- Alternative: leave the route unexplained. That's simpler, but the final display loses its "along the bridge lines" logic.

### 5. Should the original creature be destroyed on screen?
- **(rec.) No.** It's sealed alive in a vial (B33). Its fate stays unknown.

### 6. Ila's fate
- **(rec.) Taken away. Nothing more shown.** No detention scenes, no voice messages.

### 7. Runtime
- **(rec.) ~16:30** for the full beat sheet. A 12-minute cut is defined in the beat sheet.

### 8. Timeline
- **(rec.) ~25 days in story time**, with Day 0 at burial and the coda around Day 25. Shown only through the log, the plant and the panel. **No date cards.**

## B. Character

### 9. Protagonist's name
- **(rec.) Ila** (Sanskrit for "earth"). It's never spoken, but it appears in credits and press.
- Alternatives: Noor (light), Mara, Wren, Asha, Sera.
- Also open: does she need a surname? On screen the system only ever calls her **41-786-12**, which is a deliberate choice.

### 10. Ila's look & casting
- Open: ethnicity, face and casting direction. **This needs to be locked before the first character generation,** so she stays consistent across shots.
- Wardrobe and respirator are proposed in Protagonist → *Canonical look*.

### 11. The pet
- **(rec.) Feline pattern.** It's the most universally readable "pet" silhouette and the clearest contrast to the creature.
- Name: open. Suggestions: **Sable**, Pim, Juno. It can also simply go unnamed, with only a licence code.

### 12. The coda household
- **(rec.) We see only the child, the plant and a handwritten label.** The adult who grows it stays off screen.
- Optional: the label's handwriting style echoes grandmother's diagram, hinting at an old network of people who kept trying. It's subtle, and it rewards a rewatch.

## C. World

### 13. City name and cultural setting
- **(rec.) Unnamed city, globally readable.** It's specific in texture but not in nationality.
- Alternative: a named future city (for example a vertical Mumbai, Singapore or Shenzhen). This gives a stronger identity and grounds the design, but narrows the reading.
- Note: the earlier IMAGO draft leaned South Asian (Peda, Titli). Flutter keeps a trace of that in Ila's name.

### 14. The plant
- **(rec.) Wild strawberry.** It rhymes with the protein "strawberry" from Beat 2, and a tiny green berry is a clear "something new."
- Alternatives: tomato (needs vibration to pollinate, which is scientifically strong but visually less iconic) or a bean (fast-growing, very humble).

### 15. The Authority's name
- **(rec.) Biological Preservation Authority, called "Preservation" in everyday speech, with the motto *LIFE, PROTECTED.***

### 16. The founding catastrophe's name
- **(rec.) The Remedy** (the engineered organisms) and **the Collapse** (what followed). It's never named on screen except as *REMEDY-CLASS* on the ops display.

## D. Creature

### 17. Production nickname
- Open. It's never used on screen. Suggestions: **"Moth"** (the crew will call it that anyway), "Imago," "Titli," "41-A."

### 18. Colour
- **(rec.) No emitted light, and no colour of its own** in Generation 1. Faint rose-gold iridescence at angles only. Generation 2 has the first dusk-blue pigment.
- This is a deliberate break from the IMAGO draft's glowing blue butterfly.

### 19. Asymmetry
- **(rec.) A right-wing kink with a dip to the right in flight**, inherited by Generation 2. It's the visual link between the original and the coda.

## E. Sound & music

### 20. Score
- **(rec.) No score.** The only sound design is breath, wings, hum and interface tones. If anything, one sustained tone under the coda (B36–38).
- Alternative: a minimal score. It risks softening the silence of Beat 28.
