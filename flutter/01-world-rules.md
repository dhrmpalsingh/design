# FLUTTER — World Rules

**Status:** Proposed lock v0.1. Everything here is canon unless it's listed in [`05-decisions.md`](05-decisions.md).

> Humanity did not end. It adapted successfully to a world that can no longer support ordinary human life.

This is **not a dystopia of poverty. It is a dystopia of perfection.** People are comfortable, safe, well fed and well housed. The horror comes slowly, as the audience realises what was given up to get there.

---

## 1. The Law

Every living thing in the city belongs to exactly one of three legal categories. **This is the philosophical engine of the film.**

| Category | Definition | Examples | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LICENSED** | Living or semi-living things that **cannot reproduce**. They are registered, networked and have a set lifespan. | Bio-synthetic pets, simulated trees, decorative moss walls, the pollination units, humans | Legal |
| **CONTAINED** | Real biology that reproduces, but **only inside a sealed Preservation facility**. | Fungal protein farms, synthetic agriculture floors, seed vaults, microbial cultures | Legal **inside** the facility only |
| **AUTONOMOUS** | Anything that can reproduce **outside** a facility. | — (nothing, officially) | **Cannot be allowed to exist** |

The Authority's founding principle, carved into every Preservation building:

> **NOTHING LIVING MAY REPRODUCE OUTSIDE CONTAINMENT.**

The consequence for the story:
- A moss patch in your cabinet is **unlicensed biology**: a fine and a confiscation, the equivalent of a parking ticket.
- A plant that **flowers** is a reproductive event. That's serious.
- An organism that **pollinates**, helping *other* things reproduce, is **the worst thing that can exist.** It turns one breach into many.

**The system doesn't fear life. It fears reproduction.** That one sentence explains every escalation in Part IV.

## 2. History: why the Authority is rational

The Authority has to be *right* about something, or the film is just "evil government vs. cute bug."

**Around 70 years ago:** decades of ecological decline (heat, soil exhaustion, pollinator collapse, water shortage).

**Around 60 years ago: THE REMEDY.** Engineered organisms were released to fix failing farmland: a self-replicating soil microbe and engineered pollinators designed to restore crop yields. They worked. Then they kept reproducing. They hybridised with wild species, overwhelmed native microbiomes and carried pathogens across continents. Crop systems failed in cascade.

**Around 55 years ago: THE COLLAPSE.** Contaminated soil, poisoned water, mutated pathogens and famine. Humanity retreated into sealed vertical cities.

**Around 50 years ago:** The **Biological Preservation Authority** was founded with a reasonable mandate: *never again let living things reproduce beyond our control.*

**The rhyme the film depends on:** the world was ended by **an engineered organism that helped nature and then reproduced without permission.** Sameer's creature is *exactly that profile*. From the Authority's point of view, he hasn't made a miracle. He has rebuilt the thing that ended the world.

The film should never tell the audience who is right. It has to be possible to watch the final scene as **hope** or as **outbreak**.

## 3. The Biological Preservation Authority

| | |
|---|---|
| **Public name** | Preservation (people rarely say the full name) |
| **Motto** | **LIFE, PROTECTED.** |
| **Visual identity** | A single seed shape inside a circle. White on soft stone. It looks like a wellness brand, not a police force. |
| **Controls** | Oxygen allocation, water, fertile soil, seeds, DNA samples, microorganisms, all viable organic material, all Contained facilities |
| **How control drifted** | It started as protection: whoever controlled biology prevented a second Collapse. Over time it became the obvious corollary: whoever controls oxygen, water and biology controls civilisation. Nobody decided to become a tyranny. The rules simply never relaxed. |

### Enforcement

- **No soldiers. No guns.** Enforcement is done by **Preservation Officers**: biologists and technicians in white sealed suits with soft voices and careful hands. They treat a violation like a contamination event, because to them that's what it is. They are frightening because they're *clean*, not because they're violent.
- **Drones:** Small, quiet, white or pale stone, rounded. They look like home appliances. Some carry soft scanning lights.
- **The building itself:** Every residential tower runs on Preservation-certified systems. In a containment event, the building *is* the enforcement (see §10).

## 4. The City

- No city name is needed on screen (see Decisions).
- **Towers rise 400 to 1,000 floors** into a permanently polluted atmosphere. People rarely touch the ground; most have never been there.
- Towers are connected by **aerial bridges** (enclosed glass walkways and plazas), **transport tubes** (pressurised rail between towers) and **flying commuter vehicles** (quiet, driverless, in managed traffic lanes).

### Elevation is class

| Band | Floors | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | 0–150 | Sealed industry, recycling, water processing. Workers only. |
| **Lower** | 150–400 | Dense, functional, older systems. |
| **Mid** | 400–700 | The working middle. Transit concourses. |
| **Upper** | 700–900 | Comfortable professionals. Better light, quieter air. **Sameer lives here (Floor 786).** |
| **Summit** | 900+ | Wealth. Private flyers, real-glass skylights, the closest thing to sky. |

Sameer is **not poor**. He lives in a nice apartment in a beautiful building. This matters: he has a lot to lose, and the system has been good to him.

### The shared air network ⚠ (critical for the ending)

Every tower's exhaust air goes up through **shared risers** into an **inter-tower air exchange** that runs *through the aerial bridges*, where it's filtered, rebalanced and redistributed. The bridges aren't just walkways. They are the city's lungs.

This is how the ending works: whatever gets into one tower's exhaust can reach another tower. On the final display, new detections appear **along the lines of the bridges**, so the audience can see the route without anyone explaining it.

## 5. Air & Breathing

- **Controlled interiors** (homes, offices, transit cabins) are fully conditioned. No apparatus is needed inside them.
- **Everywhere else** (corridors between zones, bridges, platforms, public plazas) requires a **respiratory apparatus**.
- **Windows do not open.** Not anywhere, not ever. There are no handles, and windows are a single sealed pane.
- **Clean air is metered.** Every resident has an **oxygen allocation**. It's displayed on home panels and the apparatus. Allocation can be **reduced** as a compliance measure.
- **Children learn to attach their unit the way children today learn to tie their shoes.** It's a rite of passage, and nobody comments on it.

### The respiratory apparatus: two tiers

What you breathe through says what you do. Both tiers **collect condensation** from the wearer's breath into a removable reservoir, and both **attach with a click** and a brief seal-check tone. That sound is a motif.

**Consumer tier (almost everyone):**
- A **compact, elegant consumer product.** Think premium earbuds or a luxury watch, not a gas mask.
- A slim crescent unit that sits along the jaw and under the nose, with a near-invisible clear seal over the nose and mouth. The face stays readable.
- A tiny **status light** at the temple for seal and allocation.

**Work tier (technicians, Base workers, filtration crews; this is Sameer's):**
- **Locked from the protagonist reference (C03).** A **grey half-face mask with twin side filter cartridges**, a **black corrugated hose** over the left shoulder, and a **compact grey belt unit** at the left hip that holds filtration, allocation and the **condensation canister**.
- Built for ducts, wall cavities and dirty plant rooms, where the consumer tier would clog.
- It's functional, heavy and slightly old-fashioned. **Among the crescents in a lift, it marks him as someone who works inside the walls.**
- **The hose is a story object.** Because his breath runs through a detachable hose, he can unclip it and **route his breath straight into the habitat** (B11, B21c).

## 6. Food

- Engineered protein, fungal farm products (Contained) and synthetic agriculture output.
- Food is dispensed at home from a wall **dispenser** in pleasant, idealised shapes. **It is shaped like fruit it doesn't taste like.** A pale protein "strawberry" is the first food we see.
- **Real fruit is something people know from images.**
- Organic waste goes into the apartment's **sterilisation port**: a small wall hatch that flashes heat and UV and leaves nothing behind. Every home has one. *(It becomes the site of Sameer's final choice.)*

---

## 7. Pets

People keep pets, and the pets are **LICENSED**:
- **Bio-synthetic companions:** warm, soft, responsive. Engineered tissue over processors, with artificial nervous systems.
- They behave like cats, birds, dogs, even butterflies, **without belonging to any ecosystem.** They don't eat prey, shed, age naturally or breed.
- **Sterile by design. Fixed lifespan. Annual licence renewal.**
- **Networked.** Their eyes and ears are part of the home's sensor suite. Most owners don't think about that. Sameer, who installs the sensors, does.
- Their behaviour is **scheduled and perfect**: they wake on time and move in clean, repeating patterns.

---

## 8. Plants & Trees

- **Trees exist primarily as simulations:** projected canopies, moulded synthetic trunks, moss walls of licensed non-reproducing tissue. Public plazas are full of greenery, and none of it is alive in the way that matters.
- **Real plants exist only in CONTAINED facilities:** sealed agricultural floors where crops are grown under total control.
- In those facilities, crops are pollinated by **pollination units**: tiny bio-synthetic insect-drones with carbon-fibre wing spars and self-repairing engineered tissue. They're sterile and centrally controlled, and they fly in **perfect grid patterns**. Damaged units are swept into disposal trays. *(Sameer takes one.)*

---

## 9. Water & Rain

- Water is a regulated resource, metered per residence. Unusual consumption is flagged.
- **Rain is contaminated.** It falls warm and faintly amber, streaks the sealed glass, and is collected by the Base for processing. People don't go out in it; bridges close their open sections, and drones rinse surfaces afterwards.
- The atmosphere is permanently hazy, and **beautiful**: particulates turn the light into an endless rose-amber golden hour. The sun is a pale disc. *The sky is always gorgeous, and that's the problem.*

## 10. Surveillance

### The residence sensor suite

Every apartment continuously measures:

| Sensor | What it detects |
|---|---|
| O₂ / CO₂ | Oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide balance (plants change both) |
| Humidity | Moisture beyond occupant baseline |
| Water | Flow, usage and evaporation |
| Particulate | Airborne particles: dust, spores, **pollen** |
| Microbial | Bacterial and fungal activity on surfaces and in the air |
| Acoustic / micro-motion | Small movements not matching licensed occupants or pets |
| Optical | Room cameras (privacy-blurred for humans; not for "unidentified" things) |
| Companion feed | The pet's own senses |

### Biological Variance

A single number: **how far a residence's biological signature deviates from its licensed baseline** (occupants and licensed pets). It's shown on every home panel. Nobody looks at it, because it's always **0.000%**.

### The threshold ladder (lock these numbers)

| Reading | System response |
|---|---|
| **0.000–0.009%** | WITHIN TOLERANCE: auto-dismissed (skin, breath and cooking produce noise) |
| **0.010–0.049%** | FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: an algorithm watches the trend |
| **≥ 0.050%** or **UNIDENTIFIED ORGANIC MOTION** | PRIORITY REVIEW: a human looks |
| **Any reproductive signature** (pollen, spores, viable seed, eggs) | **CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL**: bypasses all thresholds |

The last row is the key one. **Reproduction skips the queue.** A huge moss wall might only get a fine. One grain of pollen triggers containment.

### Residential Containment Protocol

This is the ordered escalation used in Part V. **Every step is rational containment, not cruelty**, except Step 5, which is the moment protection becomes coercion.

| Step | Action | Official justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Compliance notice** on all home interfaces | Inform the occupant |
| 2 | **Residence lock**: doors seal | Prevent spread via occupant movement |
| 3 | **Vertical isolation**: elevators halt across affected floors | Prevent spread via transit |
| 4 | **Air isolation**: ventilation sealed, the hum stops | Prevent airborne spread through the shared network |
| 5 | **Allocation reduction**: occupant oxygen lowered | "Encourage cooperation" |
| 6 | **Interface lockdown**: dispenser, lights and controls locked; the pet switches to sensor mode | Preserve the scene |
| 7 | **Drone sweep**: corridors, ducts, exterior scan lights | Locate the organism |
| 8 | **Sterilisation offer**: the occupant may destroy the organism via the sterilisation port for restored access | Voluntary compliance |
| 9 | **Officer entry**: sealed suits, sampling, removal | Containment |

## 11. Look & Feel Rules

**Avoid the Blade Runner vocabulary entirely:** no grime in the city, no neon, no rain-soaked alleys, no holographic ad clutter, no industrial decay. (The single exception is technicians' workwear, below.)

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Overall** | Beautiful, luxurious, efficient, **desirable**. The audience should want to live here, at first. |
| **Interior palette** | Warm ivory, pale stone, brushed champagne metal, frosted glass, soft sage, wood-grain composites |
| **Exterior palette** | Rose-amber haze, pale gold light, silhouetted towers in soft gradients, glints of glass |
| **Interior light** | Soft, diffused, near shadowless. Artificial daylight that changes on a schedule. |
| **⚠ The light rule** | **The air is so filtered that nothing floats in it. Light beams are invisible.** No dust motes, ever. When the creature first flies through the lamp, its shed scales and pollen make a light beam **visible for the first time in the film.** |
| **⚠ The life rule** | **No birds, no insects, no weeds, no stains, no decay** anywhere, until the habitat. The audience should feel the absence before they can name it. |
| **⚠ The one exception: workwear** | **The city is spotless; the people who clean it are not.** Technicians' work clothes carry dust and filter residue from inside the walls. Sameer's dusty charcoal jacket, cargo trousers and boots (reference C01–C03) are the **only dirt on screen** in public spaces. The dirt is on fabric, never in the air, and never on the city's surfaces. |
| **Architecture** | Elegant curves, biophilic design *without biology*: Gardens by the Bay supertrees, Zaha Hadid curves, Apple Park calm |
| **Sound** | Engineered and pleasant: soft chimes, a constant low ventilation hum, quiet magnetic clicks. **No un-designed sound** until the creature's wings. |
| **Lineage** | *Gattaca*, *Her*, *Ex Machina*, *Never Let Me Go*, *Severance*, *THX 1138*: the clean-dystopia tradition, not the cyberpunk one |

## 12. Rules → on-screen proof

The film has **no exposition**. Every rule has to be taught by an image. If a rule has no proof shot, it doesn't need to be in the film.

| Rule | How the audience learns it | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| Interiors are perfect and controlled | The apartment wakes up: light, temperature, the panel reading 21.0% O₂, 45% RH | 1 |
| Biological Variance exists | The panel shows **0.001%, WITHIN TOLERANCE**, in the corner, unremarked | 2 |
| Food imitates nature | A pale protein "strawberry" from the dispenser | 2 |
| Organic waste is sterilised | He drops a crumb into the sterilisation port; a flash; nothing left | 2 |
| Breathing is technological and normal | The magnetic click and seal tone at his door; a child in the lift doing it without help | 3–4 |
| Vertical city, elevation as class | The lift counter dropping from 786; the window reveal | 4 |
| Pets are synthetic and licensed | A dog in the lift with a licence light at its collar, moving in a slightly too-perfect rhythm | 4 |
| Trees are simulations | Commuters crossing a plaza of perfect projected trees | 5 |
| Rain is contaminated | Amber rain on a bridge; people hurry, a section seals, a drone rinses | 5 |
| Real plants exist only in facilities | His service call to a Contained agricultural floor | 6 |
| Pollination is robotic | Units flying in perfect grids; a damaged one falls | 6 |
| Even mould is policed | His work tablet: *MICROBIAL BLOOM, FOOD RESIDUE, NOTICE ISSUED* at a neighbour's unit | 6 |
| Reproduction is the real crime | The escalation in Part IV: pollen detection jumps straight past every threshold | 22 |
| Authority fear is rational | The ops-floor screen: *PROFILE MATCH, REMEDY-CLASS ORGANISM* | 23 |
| The bridges are shared lungs | The final display: detections spreading along bridge lines | 38 |
