# IMAGO — Treatment

> *imago* (n.) — the final, winged stage of an insect's metamorphosis.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Live-action / CG hybrid short film |
| **Runtime** | 17–20 minutes |
| **Genre** | Speculative drama / eco sci-fi thriller |
| **Tone** | Quiet, intimate, oppressive, ending on fragile hope |
| **Comparable works** | *Blade Runner 2049* (scale), *WALL·E* (a single living thing as hope), *Children of Men* (handheld urgency), *Okja* (protecting a creature from a system) |

---

## Logline

In a choked vertical megacity where every breath is rationed and every living thing is property of the state, a synthetic-tree technician secretly builds a half-living, half-machine butterfly — the first pollinator in a generation — and must hide it from the very surveillance masks that keep her alive.

## Tagline

**They rationed the air. She made something that could make more.**

---

## The Dream (origin)

The film is born from a dream, and it begins and ends inside one.

A woman stands barefoot in a golden meadow. No mask. Wind in the grass. She breathes all the way in, the way no one has breathed in forty years. A blue butterfly lands on her finger — and her breath turns into the hiss of a respirator valve.

She wakes on floor 612.

## Synopsis

### Act I — The Rationed Breath

The city of **VERTA** grew up instead of out. Towers of 400 to 1,000 floors pierce a brown smog sea, stitched together by thousands of skybridges called **the Lattice**. Commuters stream between towers in flying transit pods. Below floor 200 lies **the Murk**, where the air is poison. Above floor 800 sits **the Crown**, where the rich live under clean-air domes and see the sun.

Everyone wears a **breather** — a mask that filters the air, meters each person's daily ration of breaths, and quietly reports their breathing rate to the **Allocation Authority**. Fear makes you breathe faster. The mask knows when you are afraid.

Food is grey nutrient paste. Real plants are illegal "unlicensed biomass" — any oxygen they make, or water they drink, is classed as theft from the public allotment. In their place stand the **PEDA** units: towering synthetic trees in every atrium that exhale metered oxygen with a soft chime. Pets are android-biological too; nothing is born any more, it is manufactured.

**IRA (34)** maintains the Peda trees. She knows better than anyone that they are machines in plastic bark. At home, behind the water heater, she keeps a crime: a hidden garden of moss, a fern, and a single tomato plant that flowers but never fruits — because nothing is left to pollinate it.

She lives with her son **TAVI (8)**, who has never touched grass, and next door to **OREN (74)**, a former entomologist from the shuttered Bio-Archive. Together they've been chasing an impossible idea: preserved wing cells from her grandmother's resin-cased **Blue Mormon butterfly**, cultured in a jar, grown onto a micro-machine frame. A pollinator. Plants are half a sentence, Oren says. The other half had wings.

The project is missing a heart.

### Act II — The Making

Down the hall, Wardens raid a neighbour's tray of basil. **INSPECTOR SEL KADER**, a tired, quiet Authority officer, logs the arrest and nods at Ira on his way out. Her mask's breath meter climbs; she forces it down, counting.

Tavi's robotic pet sparrow, **Chirp**, dies — its power cell spent, and they can't afford another. When Tavi overhears that the butterfly needs a heart, he brings his dead sparrow to the table. *He'd want to fly again.* Chirp's tiny vibrating pump becomes the butterfly's heart.

The work keeps getting interrupted: humidity alarms, surprise air audits, Sel at the door noticing the air is "a little wet." Wings that won't open. Oren's hands that won't stop shaking. A whole culture lost when they have to flush everything down the drain during an inspection.

Then, one night, it wakes. Living blue colour floods the wings. It stumbles, flexes, lifts. It lands on the tomato flower, and Chirp's heart **buzzes** — the exact vibration tomato flowers need to release pollen, something no butterfly in nature could ever do. The machine half makes it a better pollinator than nature's. Tavi names it **TITLI** — "butterfly."

Weeks later, the plant bears a single red tomato. Three people share three slices. Tavi lifts his mask for one second to eat, and tastes something real for the first time in his life.

### Act III — The Fight

Titli slips out of the wall and flutters against the sunlit window. A passing Warden drone registers something impossible: **UNCLASSIFIED BIOSIGNATURE — FAUNA.** At Authority Command, Sel stares at the alert. *There's no fauna.* — *There is now.*

Drones swarm floor 612. Ira seals Titli in a jar and flees through the service ducts with Tavi.

Oren sees the drones and does the only thing he can: he tears open his own wall, revealing a lifetime's secret jungle. Every sensor on the floor screams. Then he hammers on his neighbours' doors — *Open them. All of them.* One by one, hidden pots and trays come out of hiding. On the Authority map, floor 612 floods red. Then 611. Then 613. *How many, sir? … All of them.* The Wardens scatter. Oren is taken, smiling, breathing the air of his own garden.

On an open skybridge, a Warden locks onto the jar. Ira does the unthinkable: she pulls the filter from her own mask and hides Titli inside it, against her face, masking its tiny breath with her own. She breathes raw city air to keep it alive. The drone reads only: **HUMAN.**

In a crowded transit pod, coughing, her meter red, Ira finds Sel sitting opposite her. He sees a flicker of blue behind her visor. Long silence. Then he places a spare filter cartridge in her hand. *Your filter's failing, citizen.* He gets off at the next stop.

Ira staggers into the great atrium of Tower 9 and uses her technician's access to climb the tallest Peda tree. Its trunk holds real soil; its canopy exhales so much oxygen and humidity that sensors can't see anything else. She releases Titli into the plastic leaves — hidden inside the Authority's proudest symbol. Then she collapses. The Wardens take her. Tavi watches from the crowd. Sel scans the canopy: **PEDA OUTPUT NOMINAL.** He looks at Tavi. He walks away.

### Epilogue — Imago

Weeks later. Tavi stands beneath the Peda after school. Among the plastic leaves: a real green shoot. A chrysalis, glowing faint blue. Then three. A new butterfly breaks free — smaller, softer, more living than machine. Something Ira never programmed. Life taking the machine back.

A voice message arrives on Tavi's wrist from a detention block. Ira, hoarse: *Did it fly?*

Tavi whispers: *They did.*

That night, Tavi dreams of the meadow. His mother is there. The air is full of wings. He breathes all the way in.

Cut to black. One real breath.

**IMAGO.**

## Themes

- **Breath as currency and as control.** The device that keeps you alive is the device that watches you. Fear is measurable.
- **Machine vs. nature is a false war.** Titli works *because* it is both. The final image is life reclaiming the machine, not destroying it.
- **Small acts become collective acts.** Ira thinks she is alone. Floor 612 proves she never was.
- **Giving your breath.** The climax is literal: a mother gives the air in her lungs to keep a fragile future alive.
- **The system hides in plain sight — and so can resistance.** The hope survives inside the regime's own monument.

## Why now

Air quality alerts, collapsing pollinator populations, and normalised surveillance are not science fiction. *Imago* pushes each one a few decades forward and asks a small, human question: what would you risk to keep one living thing alive?
