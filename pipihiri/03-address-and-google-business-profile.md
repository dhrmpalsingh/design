# Fix 3 and 4: One address everywhere, plus a Google Business Profile

## Fix 3: Make the address consistent

**Problem:** Different versions of your address are online:

- **Justdial:** Plot No. 213, Janakpuri, Delhi
- **Your site and Kala Curry:** "our Najafgarh studio"
- **Another indexed version:** a Najafgarh-Dauralla Road address that seems to include a parent's name ("S/o …") and a councillor's office as a landmark

Google checks whether your name, address and phone number match across sites. When they don't, it trusts the business less, especially in Maps.

### Decide once

Write your public business details here, then use exactly the same text everywhere:

```
Pipihiri
[Street / plot]
[Area], New Delhi [PIN]
[Phone]
```

Use the address where pottery classes happen and where people can visit. If people can't visit, use the official address and hide it on Google (see below).

## Fix 4: Create a Google Business Profile

This is the main way people find pottery classes ("pottery classes near me", Google Maps). Reviews on the profile also appear in Google search.

### Steps (about 20 minutes, plus verification)

1. Go to **business.google.com** and sign in with the Google account the business will own.
2. **Business name:** `Pipihiri` (exactly this, without extra keywords; Google can suspend profiles that stuff keywords into the name).
3. **Category:** the primary category should match what most visitors come for. If that's mostly classes, type "pottery" and choose the closest class or school category. If it's mostly buying, choose **Pottery store**. Add secondary categories for the others, such as **Gift shop**, **Corporate gift supplier** and **Art school**, if Google offers them.
4. **Location:** if people visit for classes, show the studio address. If not, choose "I deliver goods and services to my customers" and hide the address.
5. Add your phone, `https://pipihiri.com/` and opening or class hours.
6. **Verify.** Google usually asks for a short video showing the signboard or studio, your tools or stock, and proof you manage the place. Have the studio ready before you start.
7. After verification, add:
   - 15 or more photos: the studio, a class in progress, finished pieces and the team
   - **Products:** your top 8–10 pieces, with links to the site
   - **Services:** each workshop with its price (for example Beginner Pottery Workshop, 3 hrs, ₹2,500)
   - A **Post** announcing Diwali 2026 orders, linking to `/diwali-gift-ideas/`

### Getting the first reviews

- Ask everyone who finishes a class to leave a review before they leave. Put a QR code linking to your review page on the studio wall. Google gives you the link under "Ask for reviews".
- Send the same link to your corporate clients after delivery.
- Never offer discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews. It's against Google's rules and can get reviews removed.
